Do I Need to File Taxes?

The requirement to file taxes depends on your status and income level:

  • International Students: Must file Form 8843 regardless of income if physically present in the US
  • US Citizens/Permanent Residents: Generally must file if income exceeds $12,550
  • Dependents: Different thresholds apply if you're claimed as a dependent

Even if not required to file, you may want to file to get refunds of any withheld taxes.

Tax Information for International Students

General Requirements

  • All international students and scholars in F or J status who have been physically present in the United States for any portion of 2024 must file tax forms, even if no U.S. source income was earned.
  • International students are considered nonresidents for tax purposes for the first five calendar years they are in F-1, F-2, J-1, or J-2 status.
  • Nonresidents are taxed differently and have limited access to tax credits and deductions compared to U.S. citizens and residents for tax purposes.
  • Nonresidents may be eligible for tax treaty benefits, which U.S. citizens and a majority of residents for tax purposes cannot use.

Filing Deadlines

  • Individuals with U.S. Source Income: Tuesday, April 15, 2025
  • Individuals Physically Present in the United States in 2024 with No Income: Sunday, June 15, 2025

Required Forms

  • Form 8843: Required for all international students who were physically present in the U.S. in 2024, even if no income was earned.
  • Form 1040NR: Required for international students who earned U.S. source income.
  • State Tax Returns: May be required depending on the state of residence and income earned.

Important Note for Students with Income Under $12,550

Even if your income is under $12,550, as an international student, you are still required to file Form 8843 if you were physically present in the United States during 2024. If you had any U.S. source income (even under $12,550), you may also need to file Form 1040NR.

Georgetown-Specific Resources for International Students

  • Sprintax Tax Preparation Software: Georgetown provides free access to Sprintax for international students to prepare federal tax returns. Access codes are distributed by the Office of Global Services.
  • Tax Workshops: The Office of Global Services hosts tax workshops specifically for international students during tax season.
  • One-on-One Advising: Available through the Office of Global Services for complex tax situations.

Tax Information for US Citizens

General Requirements

  • U.S. citizens are subject to federal income tax on their worldwide income, regardless of where they live or work.
  • The requirement to file a tax return depends on your filing status, age, and gross income.
  • For most single students under 65, you must file if your gross income was at least $12,550 in 2024.
  • If you're claimed as a dependent on someone else's return, different filing thresholds apply.

Filing Deadlines

  • Federal Tax Returns: Tuesday, April 15, 2025
  • Extensions: If needed, you can request an automatic extension to October 15, 2025, but any tax owed is still due by April 15.

Required Forms

  • Form 1040: The standard federal income tax form for U.S. citizens.
  • Schedule 1-3: May be required depending on your specific financial situation.
  • Form 8863: For claiming education credits like the American Opportunity Credit or Lifetime Learning Credit.
  • State Tax Returns: Required for most states, including DC, Maryland, and Virginia.

Education Tax Benefits

As a student, you may be eligible for several tax benefits:

  • American Opportunity Credit: Up to $2,500 per eligible student for the first four years of higher education.
  • Lifetime Learning Credit: Up to $2,000 per tax return for tuition and related expenses.
  • Student Loan Interest Deduction: Deduct up to $2,500 of student loan interest paid.
  • Tuition and Fees Deduction: Deduct up to $4,000 of qualified education expenses.

Important Note for Students with Income Under $12,550

If your income is under $12,550 and you're not claimed as a dependent, you may not be required to file a federal tax return. However, you should still consider filing to receive a refund of any federal income tax withheld from your pay. If you're claimed as a dependent, you must file if your unearned income was more than $1,100 or your earned income was more than $12,550.

Georgetown-Specific Resources for US Citizens

  • Form 1098-T: Georgetown provides this form showing tuition payments and scholarship information, which you'll need for claiming education tax benefits.
  • VITA Program: The Volunteer Income Tax Assistance program may be available on campus to help eligible students file their taxes for free.
  • Financial Aid Office: Can provide guidance on how tax filing relates to financial aid eligibility.

Tax Information for Permanent Residents

General Requirements

  • Permanent residents (green card holders) are generally treated the same as U.S. citizens for tax purposes.
  • You are subject to federal income tax on your worldwide income, regardless of where you live or work.
  • For most single students under 65, you must file if your gross income was at least $12,550 in 2024.
  • If you're claimed as a dependent on someone else's return, different filing thresholds apply.

Filing Deadlines

  • Federal Tax Returns: Tuesday, April 15, 2025
  • Extensions: If needed, you can request an automatic extension to October 15, 2025, but any tax owed is still due by April 15.

Required Forms

  • Form 1040: The standard federal income tax form for U.S. citizens and permanent residents.
  • Schedule 1-3: May be required depending on your specific financial situation.
  • Form 8863: For claiming education credits like the American Opportunity Credit or Lifetime Learning Credit.
  • State Tax Returns: Required for most states, including DC, Maryland, and Virginia.

Education Tax Benefits

As a permanent resident student, you are eligible for the same education tax benefits as U.S. citizens:

  • American Opportunity Credit: Up to $2,500 per eligible student for the first four years of higher education.
  • Lifetime Learning Credit: Up to $2,000 per tax return for tuition and related expenses.
  • Student Loan Interest Deduction: Deduct up to $2,500 of student loan interest paid.
  • Tuition and Fees Deduction: Deduct up to $4,000 of qualified education expenses.

Important Note for Students with Income Under $12,550

If your income is under $12,550 and you're not claimed as a dependent, you may not be required to file a federal tax return. However, you should still consider filing to receive a refund of any federal income tax withheld from your pay. If you're claimed as a dependent, you must file if your unearned income was more than $1,100 or your earned income was more than $12,550.

Document Checklist

Here's a general checklist of documents you may need when filing your taxes:

For All Students:
  • Social Security Number or ITIN
  • Form 1098-T from Georgetown
  • Income statements (W-2, 1099, etc.)
  • Records of education expenses
  • Records of scholarship/grant amounts
  • Student loan interest statements
Additional for International Students:
  • Passport with visa page
  • I-20 or DS-2019 form
  • Entry and exit dates (I-94 record)
  • Sprintax access code (from Georgetown)
  • Tax treaty information (if applicable)
